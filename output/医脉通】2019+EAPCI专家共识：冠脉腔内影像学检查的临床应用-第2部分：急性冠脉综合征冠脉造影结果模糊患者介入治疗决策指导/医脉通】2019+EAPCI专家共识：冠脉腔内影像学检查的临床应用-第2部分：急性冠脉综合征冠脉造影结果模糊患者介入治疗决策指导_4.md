#### 图 2 The role of intravascular imaging in delineating thrombus.  
Panel I: an angiographic image of a left anterior descending artery in a patient with ST-elevation myocardial infarction presentation and anterior ST-segment elevation. A hazy filling defect is evident in the proximal segment of the vessel, highlighted by white arrow A. Optical coherence tomography image (A) demonstrates red thrombus (red arrows) with an irregular surface and adherent to the lumen, attenuating the light, and obscuring deeper structures.  
Panel II: a 45-year-old woman was admitted with chest pain and elevated cardiac troponin levels. Angiography (B) shows a subtotal occlusion of the mid-left anterior descending artery. Intravascular ultrasound (C) reveals a large echolucent mass within the lumen, consistent with thrombus (asterisk), with a thin fibrous cap (blue arrow). Optical coherence tomography (D, E) confirms the presence of red thrombus (blue arrows) with layered structure and high backscattering; the yellow asterisk indicates residual atheroma. Longitudinal OCT pullback (F) demonstrates the extent of thrombotic burden along the vessel segment (scale in mm).  
Panel III: serial intravascular imaging after aspiration thrombectomy. Angiography (G) shows improved flow but residual stenosis. Corresponding OCT cross-sections (G–I) at three locations reveal persistent thrombus (H, I) despite aspiration, with heterogeneous morphology: predominantly red thrombus (Hi, Ii) showing low signal intensity and shadowing, and some mixed components (Gi). Longitudinal OCT (bottom panel) correlates the cross-sectional findings with the axial distribution of thrombus burden (G, H, I marked on pullback).
