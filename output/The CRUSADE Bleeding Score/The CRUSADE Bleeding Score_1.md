Baseline Risk of Major Bleeding in Non–ST-segment Elevation Myocardial Infarction: The CRUSADE Bleeding Score

Sumeet Subherwal, MD, Richard G. Bach, MD, Anita Y. Chen, MS, Brian F. Gage, MD, MSc, Sunil V. Rao, MD, L. Kristin Newby, MD, MHS, Tracy Y. Wang, MD, MS, W. Brian Gibler, MD, E. Abstract  
Background—Treatments for non–ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (NSTE MI) reduce ischemic events but increase bleeding. Baseline prediction of bleeding risk can complement ischemic risk prediction for optimizing NSTE MI care; however, existing models are not well suited for this purpose.

Methods and Results—We developed (n=71,277) and validated (n=17,857) a model that identifies 8 independent baseline predictors of in-hospital major bleeding among community-treated NSTE MI patients enrolled in the CRUSADE Quality Improvement Initiative. Model performance was tested by *c* statistics in the derivation and validation cohorts and according to post-admission treatment (i.e., invasive and antithrombotic therapy). The CRUSADE bleeding score (range 1–100 points) was created by assigning weighted integers corresponding to the coefficient of each variable.
